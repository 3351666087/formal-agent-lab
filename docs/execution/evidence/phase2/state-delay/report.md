## What differs and why

- **rule** (phase-1 heuristic) treats UNKNOWN candidates as worth trying: roughly half of its turns are rejected assignments to machines the delayed belief shows free, with stalls of up to 18 turns; one seed runs the step budget out.
- **z3** re-plans from the belief after every rejection (a new plan version each time): it never stalls more than two turns, but still spends a third of its turns on rejections.
- **llm-stub** (labelled stand-in for the model planner) keeps choosing the first UNKNOWN assignment and stalls for the rest of the run: without a no-progress stop it burns the whole step budget (BUDGET_EXHAUSTED); with `no_progress_limit=20` it ends as FAILED/NO_PROGRESS after 20 turns without a state change, the stop text naming the stall — a result with a reason instead of a burnt budget. The limit is chosen above the longest stall of a strategy that still succeeds.
- **task-*** (task plans from the rule / Z3 / model generator) keep a working state: the freshest value per location plus the predicted effects of their own applied actions, and dispatch only what is applicable on it. They neither dispatch an operation twice nor retry a machine they know is busy: no rejected action, no stall, the goal in about the steps of the undelayed scenario and the lowest delay cost.
- A repeated observation request for the same locations with an unchanged world revision is declined and recorded (`OBSERVATION_REQUESTED` with `served: false`); the strategy then decides without it.
- A no-candidate turn ends the run with NO_APPLICABLE_ACTION (phase-1 behaviour, unchanged).
